## Market and value drivers
Buyer: business owner/product lead; user: their customers and team; approver: buyer or procurement. Trigger: launch, confusing website or repetitive workflow. Alternatives: in-house build, freelancer, template, existing software, defer. Need is plausible from service scope, not newly surveyed. Purchasing capability, willingness, access and demand trajectory remain hypotheses.
Dream result: launch something useful; observed acceptance must be agreed per proposal. Perceived likelihood: real case details and visible reviews, without unsupported outcome statistics. Delay: expose milestones and dependencies, do not promise an arbitrary duration. Effort: one brief and agreed review responsibilities, not a claim that the buyer has no work.

## Obstacle and solution map
| Stage / obstacle | Resolution | Condition / limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Before: unsure what to build | Consultation and written options | Paid scope agreed first; adviser capacity unknown |
| Before: uncertain cost | Written fixed scope and exclusions | Accurate requirements and integration review |
| During: invisible progress | Agreed design and working-product reviews | Buyer feedback and actual project cadence |
| During: expanding requirements | Separate change decisions and prices | Delivery owner enforces scope |
| After: dependence on agency | Code, assets, access and documentation handover | Full payment and third-party license terms |
| After: new improvement requests | Defined ongoing priorities | Monthly capacity, support and renewal agreed |

Mechanisms considered before format: diagnosis reduces uncertainty, milestone review catches misunderstanding, acceptance tests expose defects, handover reduces dependency. One-off review reduces commitment but needs buyer implementation; a build reduces buyer execution effort but needs more delivery time; ongoing work supports iteration but requires a recurring budget. These are choices, not quantified benefit claims.

## Delivery assembly and runbook
Core components: requirements, design, development, agreed integration, tests, launch and handover. Each is owned by Fortitudo subject to actual named project assignment; completion is the proposal's reviewed deliverable. Buyer owns content/access/feedback inputs. Cost and capacity estimates must be completed per proposal; no specific rate or staffing invented. No bonuses; all essential functionality stays in core scope.
Intake: review brief and existing tools. Milestones: agree scope, review design, review working build, test and accept. Support: distinguish defect correction from additional requests in actual agreement. Recovery: reproduce defect, agree severity and recovery plan, verify correction. Offboarding: settle ownership terms, provide agreed files/access/documentation. This is a proposed operating outline, not a verified staffing SLA.
Normal/stressed volume, actual bottleneck, utilization and project concurrency are unknown. Do not publish slot counts or turnaround until delivery owner validates capacity.

## Economics and enhancements
Pricing version: revision 2, quote-based; no public numerical price/currency or auto-renewal checkout. Scope, unit, payment schedule, tax, recurring term and cancellation must appear in the actual proposal. Collected-cash timing, cost, margin, support/refund exposure unknown. Reject invented premium anchors. Hypothesis: clearer scope and proof will improve qualified inquiries, not yet measured. No bonus, deadline, quantity limit or new guarantee. Risk reduction is an explicit scope/review process, not proof of success or a refund promise. Existing legal terms remain unchanged.
Names chosen for comprehension: Plan the project / Build and launch / Improve what is live. Reject branded package jargon and unsupported completion intervals.

## Scenario review
Late content: update milestones rather than promise unchanged delivery. Low adoption: inspect real journey and prioritize separately agreed improvements. Support overload: scope/response terms must be set before selling ongoing work. Failed integration: verify API feasibility before commitment, agree fallback or exclusions. Refund/dispute: existing signed terms control; do not invent a blanket refund or remedy.
