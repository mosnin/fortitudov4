# Existing-offer copy improvement

Scope: all 14 pitch decks. Visual design, service catalog, packages, acceptance criteria, FAQs and commercial boundaries remain intact. Pitch-specific editorial copy lives in deck-offer-copy.json; this does not silently rewrite the website offer records.

## Decisions

- Outcome: replace abstract delivery language with the job the buyer needs done. Observable completion remains in the original acceptance criteria.
- Obstacles: connect the three pains in each narrative to the scoped deliverables and buyer benefits. Before: unclear need or disconnected tools. During: review and permissions. After: access, documentation and handover.
- Value drivers: state the desired result; build confidence through inspectable work and acceptance checks; identify a first useful checkpoint without promising speed; explain the inputs required from the client. Do not claim an ROI multiplier.
- Delivery: retain existing scope and service variants. Explicitly offer smaller routes such as repair, a prototype or one agent when suitable. No bonus bundles, mandatory upsells or invented guarantees.
- Naming: keep all service names. Hooks describe a job or result, not prestige.
- Customer view: concise opening, problem, solution, benefits, process, acceptance, full scope, engagement options, inputs, limitations, FAQ and voluntary contact. Work link gives readers a way to inspect projects before contacting us.
- Economics: no new price, currency, discount, turnaround, capacity claim, refund or payment terms. Those remain project-specific. Actual staffing capacity, margins and willingness to pay are unknown here.

## Validation boundary

Text presence and rectangle checks validate PDF structure, not demand or delivery performance. Commercial outcome is unmeasured. The follow-up comprehension experiment is specified separately; it has not been run. Source evidence is the public catalog snapshot and exact edited copy, not a live provider read.
